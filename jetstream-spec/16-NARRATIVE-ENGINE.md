# JETSTREAM — Narrative Engine

The difference between a simulator and a world is that a world *remembers*. The narrative engine gives your billionaire life continuity — feuds, romances, obsessions, scandals that unfold over weeks and months. Events don't just happen; they become chapters.

## Core Concept

A **narrative arc** is a multi-week storyline with a beginning, middle, and end. It's woven into DMs, activity feed posts, event context, and gossip column content. Arcs are generated by Claude when conditions are met, advance naturally over sim time, and conclude in satisfying payoffs.

At any given sim-moment, 3-7 arcs are "live." Some involve the player. Some unfold in the background among friends, visible via gossip and DMs. All create the *texture* of a real social world.

## Design Principles

- **Arcs unfold over 2-12 weeks sim time** — not instant, not endless
- **Each arc has a clear shape**: Inciting incident → rising action → climax → resolution
- **Player can influence but not fully control** — this is other people's lives too
- **Arc state is stored**, not computed on demand — persistent, referenceable
- **Claude generates beats**, not full scripts — we prompt for next-beat given arc state
- **Arcs cross systems** — they can reference flights, events, properties, DMs seamlessly

## Arc Types

### Romance Arc
Two personas (or player + persona) develop romantic tension.
- Example: *Naomi and Jules* flirt across Cannes, group chat teases, Art Basel is the turning point.
- Beats: spark → rumors → public appearance → relationship status shift or implosion
- Duration: 6-12 weeks

### Feud Arc
Two personas fall out. Causes: business disagreement, social slight, romantic rival, perceived betrayal.
- Example: *Pemberton vs Okonkwo* — Pemberton made a dig in a podcast, Amara hasn't forgotten.
- Beats: incident → escalation (public shade) → turf battles at events → confrontation or thaw
- Duration: 4-10 weeks

### Obsession Arc
A persona becomes fixated on a project, person, cause, or pursuit.
- Example: *Naomi's longevity obsession* — visits a new clinic, talks about nothing else, drags friends into it.
- Beats: seed → deep dive → peak intensity → resolution (success, bust, or absorbed as new normal)
- Duration: 8-16 weeks

### Scandal Arc
Something blows up. Affair revealed. Business deal leaks. Tabloid photo surfaces.
- Example: *Dmitri's crypto fund* collapses; he's publicly humiliated, friends distance themselves.
- Beats: rumor → confirmation → fallout → reputation repair or ostracism
- Duration: 3-8 weeks

### Collaboration Arc
Two or more personas team up on something.
- Example: *Marcus and Rico* quietly working on a sports team acquisition together.
- Beats: secret discussions → leak → public announcement → success/failure
- Duration: 6-14 weeks

### Rivalry Arc
Softer than a feud — competitive jockeying for position, status, a romantic interest, or a deal.
- Example: *Alessandro vs Pierre* — both want the same gallery acquisition.
- Beats: parallel pursuit → tension at events → winner declared
- Duration: 4-8 weeks

### Loss Arc
A persona experiences grief, failure, or setback.
- Example: *Khalid's* prize thoroughbred dies unexpectedly.
- Beats: event → withdrawal → tentative return → integration
- Duration: 4-10 weeks
- **Player interaction: being supportive during these arcs yields significant friendship gains**

### Family Arc
Persona's family enters the picture — parent, child, sibling, cousin causes drama, illness, or joy.
- Example: *Elena's* father's health declines; she's in Switzerland more often.
- Beats: setup → complication → resolution
- Duration: 6-16 weeks

### Player-Centered Arc
An arc that specifically involves the player.
- Example: *Sasha invites you into an inner-circle yacht week in Porto Cervo* — a test of trust. Accepting deepens friendship; declining creates distance.
- Beats: invitation → commitment → execution → outcome
- Duration: 3-8 weeks

## Arc Generation

### When Arcs Are Created

Arcs are born from **preconditions** that Claude evaluates periodically (on app open, not more than once every 3 sim days).

Trigger conditions:
1. **Low arc count**: if fewer than 3 active arcs, consider generating one
2. **Eligible pairs/triads**: personas with rivalry/close-friend relationships who haven't been in a recent arc
3. **Major events approaching**: events within 14 days that could catalyze an arc
4. **Player actions**: specific actions (attending certain events, buying near friends, hosting) can seed player-centered arcs

### The Arc Generator Endpoint

```
POST /api/ai/generate-arc
```

```

**System prompt:**
```
You are the story director for JETSTREAM. Your job is to create a single narrative arc that will unfold over the next 2-12 weeks among this billionaire social circle.

Eligible personas (can be protagonists):

- Feels organic given these personalities and recent events

### The Beat Advancement Endpoint

```
POST /api/ai/advance-arc
```

Called when an arc's `nextBeatExpectedAt` is reached.

**Request:**
```typescript
{
  arc: NarrativeArc;
  daysSinceLastBeat: number;
  worldContext: {
    currentSimTime: string;
    participantLocations: Record<PersonaID, string>;
    upcomingEventsInvolvingParticipants: BillionaireEvent[];
    recentPlayerActions: string[];
  };
}
```

**System prompt:**
```
You are advancing the narrative arc "{arc.title}".

Arc type: {arcType}
Current stage: {currentStage}
Summary: {summary}
Expected resolution: {weeks remaining}

Previous beats:
{formatted list of beats so far}

World context:
{participant locations, upcoming events, player actions}

Generate the NEXT beat of this arc. Consider:
- Where are we in the arc shape? (inciting → rising → climax → falling → resolution)
- What naturally happens next given previous beats?
- Can an upcoming event be used as a catalyst?
- Should this be visible publicly (gossip), among participants (DMs), or just be narration?

Return JSON:
{
  beatType,
  description,
  visibility,
  newStage,            // may advance to next stage, or stay
  affectedFriendships,
  suggestedContent: {
    triggersDM: [...],
    triggersGossip: [...],
    triggersActivityPost: [...],
    triggersEventMoment: {eventId, moment}
  },
  nextBeatInDays: number,     // 3-21 typical
  isResolution: boolean       // true if this beat closes the arc
}
```

## Arc Execution

When a beat advances and returns `suggestedContent`, we execute it:

```typescript
// /lib/narrative-engine.ts

async function executeBeat(arc: NarrativeArc, beat: ArcBeat, content: SuggestedContent) {
  // Record the beat on the arc
  await narrativeRepo.addBeat(arc.id, beat);
  
  // Fire DMs
  if (content.triggersDM) {
    for (const dm of content.triggersDM) {
      await dmRepo.appendMessage(dm.threadId, {
        from: dm.fromPersonaId,
        content: dm.messageContent,
        context: { trigger: "arc_beat", relatedId: arc.id },
      });
      // Mark as arc-related for future context
    }
  }
  
  // Fire gossip items (see GOSSIP spec)
  if (content.triggersGossip) {
    for (const item of content.triggersGossip) {
      await gossipRepo.queueForNextIssue(item);
    }
  }
  
  // Fire activity feed posts
  if (content.triggersActivityPost) {
    await activityRepo.create({
      timestamp: now(),
      content: content.triggersActivityPost.content,
      arcId: arc.id,
    });
  }
  
  // Apply friendship changes
  if (beat.affectedFriendships) {
    for (const change of beat.affectedFriendships) {
      await personaStateRepo.adjustFriendship(change.personaId, change.delta);
    }
  }
  
  // Update arc state
  await narrativeRepo.update(arc.id, {
    currentStage: beat.newStage,
    nextBeatExpectedAt: addDays(now(), beat.nextBeatInDays),
    summary: content.updatedSummary,  // Claude may refresh
  });
  
  // Resolve if needed
  if (beat.isResolution) {
    await narrativeRepo.resolve(arc.id);
  }
}
```

## When the Engine Runs

Arcs are **lazy-evaluated** on app open (matching the rest of the timestamp-based sim):

```typescript
// Called from getWorldState()
async function advanceNarratives(now: Date): Promise<void> {
  // 1. Advance any arcs whose next beat is due
  const activeArcs = await narrativeRepo.getActive();
  for (const arc of activeArcs) {
    if (arc.nextBeatExpectedAt && new Date(arc.nextBeatExpectedAt) <= now) {
      const beatResponse = await callArcAdvance(arc);
      await executeBeat(arc, beatResponse.beat, beatResponse.content);
    }
  }
  
  // 2. Maybe generate a new arc (throttled)
  const lastGeneration = await narrativeRepo.lastGenerationTime();
  if (differenceInDays(now, lastGeneration) >= 3) {
    const activeCount = activeArcs.filter(a => a.status === 'active').length;
    if (activeCount < 3) {
      const newArc = await callArcGenerate();
      if (newArc) {
        await narrativeRepo.create(newArc);
        await executeBeat(newArc, newArc.firstBeat, newArc.firstBeat.suggestedContent);
      }
    }
  }
}
```

## Player Interaction with Arcs

### Visibility

- **Public arcs**: Player sees everything via gossip + activity feed
- **Participant arcs**: Player sees DMs if they're a participant, otherwise partial via gossip
- **Player-centered arcs**: Player is a protagonist; arc surfaces strongly

### Influence

Some arcs have **decision points** where the player can intervene. These surface as:

**Choice DMs**: "Tama — between us — Dmitri's been spreading that shit about your event last week. Want me to say something?" — player responds, choice affects arc trajectory.

**Event dynamics**: arriving at an event where two rival personas are present may trigger a "whose side are you on" choice.

**Hosting opportunities**: hosting an event during an active arc between participants can accelerate or defuse it.

These are surfaced as subtle prompts, not explicit "Choose A or B" menus — the game reads player action (who they DM back, where they show up) as implicit choices.

### UI Surface

New route: **`/arcs`** (or embedded in Social hub as a tab)

Displays:
- **Active arcs** the player is aware of
- Each as a card: title, participants, status, most recent beat description
- Tappable → arc detail view showing all known beats, participant portraits, a timeline
- Hidden arcs the player shouldn't know about yet are simply not shown

### Arc Detail Page

Layout:
- Title + mood badge + status
- Participants (portraits in a row)
- Summary
- Timeline of known beats (the ones the player has witnessed/heard about)
- Connected content: DMs from this arc, gossip items, events touched, relevant activity posts
- If player involved: "Your moves in this arc" section

## Integration with Existing Systems

### DMs
When a DM is part of an arc beat, it's tagged with `context.trigger = "arc_beat"` and `relatedId = arcId`. The DM thread UI shows a subtle tag: "Part of: The Monaco Feud" (tappable → arc detail).

### Activity Feed
Arc-related activity posts show a small arc indicator (faint cyan line on the side) and optionally tag with `Part of: {arc title}`.

### Events
When an event hosts an arc beat, the event's recap page notes it: "This was where the Pemberton-Okonkwo tension finally broke."

### Gossip
Gossip column pulls heavily from arc beats with `visibility: "public"`.

### Hosting
Hosting a party during an active arc can affect it. Claude receives arc context when generating hosting accept/decline responses, and hosting outcomes can include arc-advancing beats.

## Cost Considerations

Arc generation is **Sonnet-class work** — it requires world understanding, character modeling, and narrative judgment.

Estimated calls per month:
- Arc generation: 2-4 new arcs/month = 2-4 Sonnet calls
- Beat advancement: 3-7 active arcs × 1-2 beats/week = 12-30 Sonnet calls/month
- **Total: ~15-35 Sonnet calls/month** at ~$0.08 each = **$1.20-$2.80/month**

Very affordable. This is the highest-leverage Claude usage in the app.

## The "Holy Shit" Moment

Six weeks into regular play:
- You've been following the Pemberton-Okonkwo feud through gossip columns and DMs
- Amara has started DMing you more since Pemberton said something about her at Davos
- You attend Art Basel Miami. You see both of them in the same room. Tension.
- The next week, Page Six runs: "Sources say the frost between activist investor Charles Pemberton and macro fund legend Amara Okonkwo thawed over dinner at Cheval Blanc Miami — reportedly at the invitation of JETSTREAM's own Tama, who may have quietly brokered peace."

*You* didn't consciously broker peace. You just invited them both to dinner because you own in Miami during Basel. But the narrative engine *noticed* and wove it into the world.

**That's the moment you realize the game is alive.**
